## What website development actually costs in Kraków (EUR)

Here's the part you came for. Real ranges, no "it depends" cop-out:

- **Landing page (one page, one goal):** €400–900. A single page for a service, a tour, an apartment listing, an event. Fast to build, fast to rank if the niche is thin.
- **Company site (5–15 pages):** €900–3,000. Home, services, about, contact, blog. This is what most Kraków service businesses actually buy — a law firm near Plac Wszystkich Świętych, a dental clinic in Podgórze, a software house in Zabłocie.
- **Custom design + technical SEO + multilingual:** €3,000–5,000+. When you sell to the PL/EN/UA crowd, or you're a startup that needs the site to convert and not just exist.
- **Online store / booking system:** €2,500–8,000. WooCommerce, headless commerce, or a booking engine with payments — and in a tourism city that last one is half the local market.

Now the trap, and it's specific to Kraków. The corporate SSC and BPO ecosystem here — tens of thousands of people in shared-service and outsourcing centers — sets the local idea of what software costs. Day rates in those buildings on the south side of the river are tuned to London and Frankfurt budgets. So when a 12-person Kraków software house quotes your restaurant a brochure site, you're paying against that backdrop: an account manager, a designer, a project lead, and a junior dev fresh out of AGH who writes the actual code while a senior signs off and bills senior.

You run a four-room boutique hotel in the Old Town. You do not need an enterprise delivery team. You need someone who builds the thing, makes it load fast on a tourist's phone, and answers when you call.

## How long does a website take in Kraków?

Quick answer, because people genuinely ask this before anything else:

**A landing page takes 1–2 weeks. A 5–15 page company site takes 2–4 weeks. An online store or booking site takes 4–8 weeks.** Anyone promising a "professional website in 48 hours" is swapping photos into a template — no SEO foundation, no real structure, nothing that'll rank past page three.

The thing that blows up timelines is never the code. It's content. I once had a clean two-week build for a Kazimierz restaurant stretch into five, because the owner couldn't get me the menu in final form, the food photos, and the English copy — all stuck waiting on a photographer and a translator. Write your text and shoot your photos before kickoff, or pay someone to. That one decision is the difference between launching this month and launching after the summer season's already gone.

## What actually drives Google rankings (and what doesn't)

Most Kraków businesses spend their budget on the wrong half of the site. They buy animations and a video of the Wawel skyline. Then a competitor with an uglier site sits above them and they can't work out why.

Here's what Google actually rewards.

**Speed.** A tourist stands in the middle of the Rynek, sun out, deciding where to eat. She taps your search result. Three seconds. Still loading. She hits back and taps the place above you. Google logged every millisecond of that, and the bounce told it something. Core Web Vitals — LCP, INP, CLS — are a ranking signal, and most of your traffic in this city arrives on a phone, often on patchy signal in a stone courtyard. Build for that phone first.

**Local SEO.** A Google Business Profile that's genuinely filled out. `LocalBusiness` schema in the actual code. Phrasing that matches how people search — "restaurant Kazimierz," "kancelaria Kraków," "apartments Old Town Kraków." If you serve a district, name the district. Kraków's local pack is its own brawl, and most sites never even enter it because nobody set the basics.

**Clean, parseable HTML.** This is the one that's changed lately. People don't only search Google now — they ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google's own AI Overviews "where should I stay near Wawel" or "best software house in Kraków." Those models read your raw HTML. A site buried under script-rendered junk and ten plugins is hard for them to quote. A clean, static site gets cited. I've watched this play out on my own builds over the past year.

What doesn't move rankings: parallax scrolling, a hero video, a "designer" font nobody can read, and the seventeenth animation on the homepage. Put that money into speed and content.

## Next.js vs WordPress — the Kraków reality

This is the biggest technical call you'll make, and in a city this dense with developers you'll hear loud opinions both ways.

**WordPress** still earns its keep. If you've got someone in-house publishing in the admin panel every week — a tour operator pushing new itineraries, say — or your budget's at the floor, it's defensible. The catch: speed has to be forced with caching plugins, security needs constant patching, and it's weak for the AI-search reading I just described. A WordPress site nobody updates is a hacked WordPress site inside a year.

**Next.js** (what I build on) ships fast by default. Static rendering, Core Web Vitals green from day one, clean HTML that Google and the AI models both parse without choking. Hosting on Vercel is frequently free. For a company site, a landing page, a booking front-end — anything aimed at ranking — it's the stronger foundation in 2026.

Simple rule: a content-heavy site with someone publishing daily → WordPress is fine. Everything else aimed at ranking and AI search → Next.js. If you want the full scope and example builds, [see the website development service](/en/services/websites).

## What Kraków businesses overpay and underspend on

After a stack of these projects, the same pattern keeps showing up.

**Overpaid:** custom design drawn from scratch when a sharp, fast layout would convert better. A 35-page site when seven pages would do. An agency retainer for a five-page brochure. And the corporate-rate problem — a small business paying SSC-flavored day rates because the quote came from a software house that mostly sells to multinationals and prices accordingly.

**Underspent:** the boring foundations. Real technical SEO. And language — done properly, not as an afterthought. This is where Kraków is unusual. You've got millions of international tourists a year, a huge and growing Ukrainian population, the international student body across UJ and AGH, plus all the English-first corporate workers. A PL-only site quietly turns away a big slice of the people who land on it. For a hotel, a tour operator, or a Kazimierz restaurant, EN is not optional and UA is often worth it. Doing PL/EN(/UA) right is one of the highest-ROI lines on the quote, and it's the first thing people cut.

**How much does a website cost in Kraków?**
A landing page runs €400–900, a 5–15 page company site runs €900–3,000, and a custom build with proper SEO and multilingual support runs €3,000–5,000 or more. **How long does it take to build a website in Kraków?**

**Next.js or WordPress for a Kraków business?**
For a company site aimed at ranking and AI search, Next.js wins: faster by default, more secure, and cleaner for Google and AI models to read. WordPress still makes sense if you have someone publishing in the admin panel daily or a very tight budget. For most local service, hospitality, and startup sites, Next.js is the better foundation.

**Do I need a multilingual website in Kraków?**
Usually yes. Kraków runs on tourism, a large Ukrainian community, an international student body at UJ and AGH, and English-first corporate workers. A PL-only site quietly turns away a big share of your visitors. For hotels, tour operators, and Old Town or Kazimierz restaurants, English is essential and Ukrainian is often worth adding.

**What actually makes a Kraków website rank on Google?**
Speed (green Core Web Vitals), real local SEO (Google Business Profile, LocalBusiness schema, district-level phrasing like "restaurant Kazimierz" or "apartments Old Town Kraków"), and clean HTML that Google and AI search engines can read. Animations, video backgrounds, and fancy fonts do nothing for rankings. Spend the budget on the foundations, not the effects.
