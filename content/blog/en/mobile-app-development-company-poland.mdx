## Objections, handled straight

**"Apps always overrun budget and timeline."** They overrun when the scope is vague. I fix the scope, price and release date before writing code, so a change of mind becomes a clear add-on, not a surprise invoice.

**"What if Apple rejects the app?"** First submissions do sometimes bounce on something small (privacy text, a login requirement, a metadata detail). I know the common rejection reasons, submit correctly the first time, and build one or two review iterations into the plan. Rejection is a step, not a disaster.

**"Who is stuck maintaining it?"** You choose. Because you own the code and the accounts, you can keep me on a light support retainer or hand the repo to an in-house developer later. No lock-in either way.

**"Freelancer or agency?"** For a defined app with a clear goal, a senior freelancer ships a better result for less money and you talk to the builder directly. An agency makes sense when you need a large team running for years in parallel. If that is you, I will tell you honestly.

**Native or cross-platform (React Native / Flutter) - which is better?**
For roughly 90% of business apps, cross-platform React Native is the right call: one codebase for iOS and Android, one budget, one thing to maintain, and a native feel users can't distinguish. Native (Swift/Kotlin) only pays off for heavy graphics, games, or deep hardware access. I default to React Native and go native only when the app truly needs it.

An MVP takes 4-8 weeks, a standard product 8-14 weeks, and a complex app 14-24 weeks. The timeline is fixed with the scope before we start, and it includes App Store and Google Play submission, not just the build.
