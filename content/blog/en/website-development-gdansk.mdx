## What actually drives Google rankings (and what doesn't)

Most Gdańsk businesses spend their budget on the wrong half of the site. They pay for a hero video of the waterfront and a parallax scroll down Długi Targ. Then they wonder why a competitor with an uglier site sits above them in search.

Here's what Google actually rewards.

**Speed.** You're a tourist walking the promenade at Brzeźno, phone in hand, searching for somewhere to eat. You tap a result. Three seconds pass. Still loading. You hit back and tap the next one. Google logged every millisecond of that. Core Web Vitals — LCP, INP, CLS — are a ranking factor, and a slow site loses people before a word gets read. Most of your traffic is mobile, often on the patchy signal you get along the coast or on a packed SKM train between Gdańsk and Sopot. Build for that phone, not your desktop.

**Local SEO.** A Google Business Profile that's properly filled in. `LocalBusiness` schema in the code. Phrasing that matches how people actually search — "web design Gdańsk," "hotel Sopot," "freight forwarder Gdańsk port." If you serve the whole Tricity, say so, because plenty of people search "Trójmiasto" and the district names. Most sites never even enter the local pack.

**Clean, parseable HTML.** This is the one that's changed. People don't only search Google anymore — they ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google's own AI Overviews. Those models read your raw HTML. A site buried under script-rendered junk and a dozen plugins is hard for them to quote. A clean static site gets cited. I've watched it happen with my own builds over the past year.

What doesn't move rankings: parallax scrolling, a video background of the shipyard cranes, a "unique" font nobody can read, and the seventeenth animation on the homepage. Put that money into speed and content.

## Next.js vs WordPress — the Gdańsk reality

This is the biggest technical call you'll make, and in a city full of developers you'll hear strong opinions both ways.

**WordPress** still earns its keep. If you've got an in-house editorial team that lives in the admin panel and publishes weekly — or your budget sits at the low end — it's defensible. The catch: speed has to be forced with caching plugins, security needs constant patching, and it's weak for the AI-search reading I described. A neglected WordPress site becomes a hacked WordPress site inside a year. I've cleaned up a few.

**Next.js** (what I build on) ships fast by default. Static rendering, Core Web Vitals green from day one, clean HTML that Google and the AI models both parse without choking. Hosting on Vercel is often free. For a company site, a landing page, or anything built to rank, it's the stronger foundation in 2026.

Simple rule: content-heavy site with a daily-publishing team → WordPress is fine. Everything else aimed at ranking and AI search → Next.js. If you want the full scope and example builds, [see the website development service](/en/services/websites).
