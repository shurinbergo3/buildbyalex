A website redesign is worth it when the site is quietly costing you leads: it loads slowly, breaks on phones, looks dated enough to hurt trust, or sits on a platform you can no longer maintain. A cosmetic **refresh** runs about €500-1,500, a real **redesign** €2,000-6,000, and a **full rebuild** on a modern stack €5,000-15,000+. The one rule that decides whether it pays off: don't lose the SEO you already have.

I rebuild sites on Next.js and migrate businesses off aging WordPress and page builders, so I'll lay this out with numbers and the trade-offs a sales pitch skips.

## Signals you actually need a redesign

Not every ugly site needs a rebuild, and not every pretty one is fine. These are the symptoms that actually justify spending money:

- **Slow load, poor Core Web Vitals.** If LCP is above 2.5s and INP is in the red in Search Console, you're losing both rankings and buyers before the page even paints.
- **Not mobile-friendly.** More than half of traffic in Poland comes from phones. A layout that pinches, overflows, or hides the CTA on mobile is a hole in your revenue.
- **Traffic but no leads.** People arrive and leave. The offer is buried, the CTA is weak, the form is a chore. This is a conversion problem, not a color problem.
- **A dated look that costs trust.** Design from 2014 tells a visitor "this business might be gone." Fair or not, it shows up in bounce rate.
- **An unmaintainable CMS.** Thirty plugins, a theme that can't be updated without breaking, security warnings, a developer who left. When editing anything is scary, the platform is the problem.
- **You can't add what you need.** Booking, multi-language, a payment step, a proper blog - and the current setup fights you every time.
- **A rebrand.** New name, logo, or positioning that the old site no longer reflects.

One or two of these means a targeted fix. Four or more usually means the foundation is done.

## Redesign vs refresh vs rebuild

The most expensive mistake is buying a full rebuild when a refresh would have done, or slapping new paint on a broken foundation. Match the fix to the symptom:

| Symptom | Right fix | Rough cost |
|---|---|---|
| Look feels dated, but speed and structure are fine | **Refresh** - new styling, fonts, images, hero | €500-1,500 |
| Poor mobile, weak Core Web Vitals, content is OK | **Front-end redesign** | €2,000-5,000 |
| Traffic but no leads, unclear offer, messy structure | **Conversion redesign** - new IA, copy, CTAs | €2,500-6,000 |
| Old CMS, dead plugins, security risk, can't add features | **Full rebuild** on a modern stack | €5,000-15,000 |
| Rebrand across name, logo, positioning | **Redesign or rebuild** (depends on the tech) | €2,000-12,000 |

A **refresh** keeps the same pages, URLs, and platform and changes how it looks. A **redesign** reworks layout, structure, and often the copy while keeping the content. A **rebuild** replaces the underlying tech entirely, usually because the old one can't be saved.

## Don't lose your SEO

This is where redesigns go wrong. A team ships a beautiful new site, and three weeks later organic traffic is down 60%. Google didn't punish the design - it lost the map. Before touching anything, protect what already ranks.

- **Keep the URL structure, or 301-redirect old to new, one to one.** Every URL that has traffic or backlinks needs a permanent redirect to its new equivalent. Not to the homepage - to the matching page.
- **Migrate content and metadata.** Titles, meta descriptions, H1s, image alt text, and structured data all carry ranking signals. Moving the design but dropping the copy throws away years of work.
- **Check what ranks before you cut it.** Pull your top pages from Search Console. The page that looks old to you may be the one bringing half your leads. Redesign it, don't delete it.
- **Don't accidentally noindex.** Staging sites are blocked from Google on purpose. The classic disaster is launching with that block still in the code.
- **Launch the redirect map with the site, then watch.** Submit the new sitemap, keep an eye on Search Console coverage and 404s for a month, and fix what slips.

## What actually moves the needle

Owners ask for "a modern look." What they actually need is more leads, and visuals are only part of that. In order of impact:

1. **A clear offer above the fold.** In three seconds a visitor should know what you do, for whom, and what to do next. Most redesigns win here before a single pixel of styling.
2. **Speed.** LCP under one second keeps people on the page and lifts rankings. On a modern stack this is a default, not an upsell.
3. **One obvious next step per page.** One primary CTA, not five competing buttons. A form that asks for three fields, not ten.
4. **Trust signals in the right places.** Real cases, real reviews, a face and a name. This is what turns a visitor into a message.
5. **Mobile-first, genuinely.** Designed for the phone first, then scaled up - not a desktop layout squeezed down.

A prettier site with the same buried offer converts the same. If you're paying for a redesign, [my website builds](/en/services/websites) are scoped around conversion and speed, not just a fresh coat.

## Checklist: should you redesign now?

Redesign now if three or more of these are true:

- You get traffic but almost no leads or calls.
- Core Web Vitals are red in Search Console.
- Bounce rate on mobile is well above desktop.
- You can't edit content yourself without a developer.
- The design makes you hesitate to send the link to a client.
- Your platform blocks a feature you actually need.
- You've rebranded and the site no longer matches.

If only one is true, a refresh or a single-page fix is smarter money. If it's mostly "I'm bored of it," wait - boredom is not a business case.

## FAQ

**How much does a website redesign cost?**
A cosmetic refresh runs €500-1,500 (2,000-6,500 zł), a real redesign €2,000-6,000 (8,500-26,000 zł), and a full rebuild on a modern stack €5,000-15,000+ (22,000-65,000+ zł). The range depends on how many pages you have, whether content is ready, and how much of the platform gets replaced.

**Will a redesign hurt my Google rankings?**
It can, and that's the most common redesign disaster. If you keep the URL structure or 301-redirect every old URL to its match, migrate titles and meta, and don't accidentally leave a noindex block in, rankings hold. Skip those steps and traffic can drop 50% or more.

**What's the difference between a refresh, a redesign, and a rebuild?**
A refresh keeps the same pages and platform and changes the look (€500-1,500). A redesign reworks layout, structure, and copy while keeping the content (€2,000-6,000). A rebuild replaces the underlying technology, usually because the old CMS can't be maintained (€5,000-15,000+).

**How long does a website redesign take?**
A refresh is 1-2 weeks, a full redesign 3-6 weeks, and a rebuild with content migration 6-12 weeks. The delay is rarely the code - it's photos, copy, and approvals. When content is ready, it moves fast.

**Do I need a redesign or just faster hosting?**
If the design is fine but the site is slow, sometimes better hosting, image optimization, or fewer plugins fixes it for a few hundred euro. If it's slow because it's built on thirty plugins and a heavy theme, hosting won't save it - that's a rebuild.

**Can you redesign the site without changing my domain and email?**
Yes. The domain, business email, and hosting stay in your name. A redesign changes what visitors see and how fast it loads, not your address. Old URLs are handled with 301 redirects so nothing you rank for is lost.
