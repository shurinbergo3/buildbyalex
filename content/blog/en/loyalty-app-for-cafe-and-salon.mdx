A paper stamp card with nine little coffee stamps lives in a wallet for about two weeks before it ends up in the wash or the bin. A plastic loyalty card does the same, just slower. Neither one sends a push at 4 PM on a slow Tuesday saying "your free latte is waiting." A **loyalty app for your cafe, restaurant or salon** does exactly that, and that single difference is why I keep building them instead of printing cards.

Here is the honest version of this article: a custom app is not always the right first move. Sometimes a 12 €/mo ready platform is the smarter start. Below I lay out both sides with real numbers so you can pick the one that fits your budget and your stage.

## Why stamp cards and plastic cards quietly fail

The problem is not the discount. The problem is that the card has no way to talk to the customer. Once it is in their pocket, you have lost contact until they happen to walk past your door again.

Three things break:

1. **No reminder.** Repeat business is mostly about being remembered at the right moment. A card cannot ping someone. An app can.
2. **No data.** You have no idea who came twice, who stopped coming, or what they buy. You are running on gut feeling.
3. **Friction.** People forget the card, lose it, or feel silly asking for a stamp on a single espresso. Drop-off is huge before the reward is even close.

What actually drives a second visit is a nudge plus a reason. "You are two stamps from a free haircut" sent as a push beats a card buried in a drawer every single time.

## The ready platform: cheap, fast, and limited

Off-the-shelf loyalty apps (the white-label kind that charge roughly 8-15 €/mo) are a real option, and I will not pretend otherwise. You sign up, upload a logo, set "buy 9 get 1," and you are live in an afternoon.

When that is genuinely the right call:

- You are testing whether loyalty even moves the needle for your place.
- Your budget right now is closer to 200 € than 2000 €.
- You have one location and a few hundred regulars.

The catch is in two words: **brand** and **data**. Your customers download an app that has the platform's name on it, lives next to fifty other businesses, and looks like a generic template. And the customer list, the visit history, the buying patterns - that sits in the platform's database. The day you want to leave, you often cannot take it with you cleanly. You are renting your own customer relationships.

## The custom loyalty app: your brand, your data, your rules

A custom app is your app. Your name on the store icon, your colours, your tone. And crucially, **you own the data** - every customer, every visit, every euro spent sits in your database, exportable any time.

What it actually does:

- **Points or stamps**, however you want them. Spend-based, visit-based, tiered (regular / VIP), bonus on birthdays.
- **Push notifications** - the real engine. "Free coffee waiting," "20% on colour this week," "we miss you, here is 15% to come back." Sent to the right segment, not blasted at everyone.
- **Your brand** front and centre, so the app feels like part of the experience, not a third-party add-on.
- **Integration with what you already run** - your POS (so points add automatically at checkout), your booking system like Booksy for a salon, BLIK or Przelewy24 for any in-app payments.

## Does push actually pay off? The math

Push is the part owners underrate. A retail or hospitality push campaign typically sees open and action rates many times higher than email, because it lands on the lock screen, not in a spam folder.

Rough but realistic: a cafe with 1500 app users sends "free pastry with any coffee today" to the 400 who have not visited in 14 days. If even 8% come in, that is 32 extra visits in a day, and most buy more than the pastry. Run that two or three times a month and the app has paid for its own monthly cost many times over - while building the habit that brings them back without a prompt.

That is the difference between a discount and a relationship. The card gives a discount once. The app builds the habit.

## What it costs and how long it takes

Straight numbers, net:

- **MVP loyalty app** (points/stamps, push, your branding, one POS or booking integration): from **6000-12000 €**, roughly **6-10 weeks** to both stores.
- **Fuller version** (tiers, segments, in-app payments, referral, analytics dashboard): **15000 €+**.
- **Ready platform**, for comparison: roughly **8-15 €/mo**, live in a day, but no real brand and no data ownership.

So the honest decision rule: if you are unsure loyalty works for you, or your budget is tight, start on a ready platform for a few months, watch whether pushes drive visits, then build custom once you have proof and a customer base worth owning. If you already know your regulars are your business and you have the budget, skip the rental and build the asset.

## FAQ

**How much does a custom loyalty app cost?**
An MVP with points, push notifications, your branding and one integration runs from 6000-12000 € net, delivered in about 6-10 weeks for both iOS and Android. A fuller version with tiers and analytics is 15000 €+.

**Is a 12 €/mo loyalty platform good enough?**
For testing the idea or on a tight budget, yes. The trade-offs are that the app carries the platform's brand, not yours, and the customer data lives in their system, so you cannot easily take it with you later.

**What actually makes customers come back?**
A reminder at the right moment plus a reason. Push notifications to people who have not visited in a while ("free coffee waiting") drive repeat visits far better than a paper card they have to remember to bring.

**Can the app connect to my POS or Booksy?**
Yes. A custom app can add points automatically at checkout through your POS and tie into a booking system like Booksy for salons, so staff do nothing extra and points are never forgotten.

**How long before it pays off?**
Most owners run two or three targeted push campaigns a month. With a user base of even 1000-1500 people, a single "come back" campaign typically brings enough extra visits to cover the app's running cost several times over.
