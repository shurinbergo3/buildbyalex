Before writing to you, a prospect asks ChatGPT: "what is company X, are they worth working with." And gets an answer you never wrote, never approved and have almost certainly never seen. For one client of mine, the model confidently reported that the company only works with enterprise customers. That was true four years ago, and it was costing them several enquiries a month.

Here is how to audit your own picture in half an hour, where models get their facts, and what you can do about it.

## Why this suddenly matters

Three reasons this stopped being a curiosity.

**The answer replaced the results page.** People used to read ten sources and form their own view. Now the view is handed to them, and it sounds confident regardless of whether it is correct.

**It is part of the buying decision.** Checking a supplier before the first contact is a natural step, and a model is more convenient than search for that.

**You will never find out.** A negative or inaccurate answer leaves no trace in your analytics. The person simply does not write to you, and you have no idea why.

## A thirty-minute audit

Open ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Claude in separate tabs and run one set of questions through all of them. Do it in every language your customers use: the answers differ substantially.

**Block 1. Direct questions about you.**
- What is [company name]?
- What does [company name] do?
- Can I trust [company name]?
- How much do [company name]'s services cost?

**Block 2. Category and geography.**
- Who does [your service] in [your city]?
- Recommend a company for [customer's job to be done] in Poland.
- Best [your niche] for small business.

**Block 3. Comparison.**
- [Your name] or [competitor]?
- Alternatives to [large competitor].

**Block 4. Objections.**
- Reviews of [company name].
- Downsides of working with [company name].

Log the answers in a spreadsheet: question, model, were you named, what was said, which links were cited. Repeat in a month to see movement.

## What you will probably find

From auditing client brands, the picture usually falls into five patterns.

**You are absent entirely.** The most common outcome for a small business. The model names three familiar companies and you are not among them. That is not a reputation problem, it is a recognition problem.

**You are known, but the facts are stale.** Old prices, services you dropped, the city you moved away from. The model draws on what got indexed, not on today's reality.

**You are being confused with someone.** A similarly named company in another country, a namesake, a brand from a different industry. Classic for short, generic names.

**You are named cautiously.** "There is a small company X, though information about it is limited." That means external corroboration is thin.

**A competitor is recommended instead.** Usually the one third parties write about more, not the one with the better website.

## Where the model gets its facts

Understanding the sources tells you what to fix.

**Your website** is the primary but not the only source. What matters is what is written as text rather than baked into images, and whether structured data exists.

**Directories and marketplaces** - profiles in industry listings, on platforms, in company registers.

**Reviews** - Google, industry platforms, social media. Models read both the score and the text.

**Third-party mentions** - articles, partner case studies, interviews, quotes in roundups. That is what builds confidence: your own site is one thing, three independent sources are another.

**Social and professional networks** - LinkedIn in particular, since it carries structured company data.

The key mechanism: **models trust consistency**. If your name, address, services and positioning match across five places, the answer comes out confident. If they differ everywhere, you get hedging or silence.

## What to do

In order, from what you fully control to what you influence indirectly.

### 1. Get your own facts straight

Your About page needs verifiable facts as text: what you do, since when, where you are based, who you serve, which services and in what price range, in which languages. Not slogans, data. The same goes into `Organization` markup with a `sameAs` list linking the site to every profile you own. How that works is in [the Schema.org breakdown](/en/blog/schema-markup-for-ai-search-2026).

Separately: if prices, services or geography changed and the site only reflects it on one page, update everywhere. The model takes whatever it finds first.

### 2. Make sure you are being let in

Basic, but check it first. If AI crawlers cannot reach the site, nothing else matters. How to check and configure it is in [the AI crawlers article](/en/blog/llms-txt-and-ai-crawlers-gptbot).

### 3. Synchronise your profiles

Google Business Profile, LinkedIn, industry directories, registers. Same name, address, phone and description everywhere. Inconsistency is the main reason a model hedges.

### 4. Collect reviews

Real ones, with text, on platforms that get indexed. A review where someone describes the problem and the outcome is worth far more than a bare score: the model quotes the text.

### 5. Show up in third-party sources

Roundups, interviews, guest pieces, case studies with clients and partners, industry platforms. This is the most laborious and the most effective: external sources are what turn "such a company exists" into "I recommend this company".

### 6. Publish material worth citing

Specifics, numbers, experience. A model prefers to cite whoever actually answers the user's question. The mechanics are in [the GEO article](/en/blog/how-to-get-cited-by-chatgpt-and-ai-overviews).

## What you cannot do

Honestly about the limits: there is no "correct the information about my company" button. Models have feedback forms, but they do not function like editing a listing. The only reliable way to change the answer is to change what it is assembled from: your site, your profiles, and what others write about you.

Which means the process is not fast. First shifts typically appear 4-8 weeks after the updated pages get indexed; a stable picture takes several months.

## How to track it from here

Keep the spreadsheet and re-run the same questions monthly. Watch three things: whether you are named, whether the facts are right, and which links get cited. Add referrals from AI sources in GA4 and the share of branded queries in Search Console: a rise in people searching your company name usually means models started recommending you.

## FAQ

**Why doesn't ChatGPT know my company?**
Usually because very little is written about it beyond your own website. Models build a picture from the combination of sources: site, directories, reviews, third-party mentions, professional network profiles. With only a website there is nothing corroborating the company's existence, so the model names businesses it has more data on. The second common cause is technical: AI crawlers cannot access the site.

**How do I fix wrong information about my company in AI answers?**
There is no direct route: models have no "edit our data" form, and feedback does not work like editing a listing. The only working path is to change the sources - update the facts on your site as text, fix your Organization markup, synchronise directory and social profiles, and gather fresh mentions and reviews. After reindexing, the answers start to shift, typically in 4-8 weeks.

**How often should I check what the models say?**
Monthly is enough for most businesses; quarterly is the minimum. Regularity matters more than frequency, because the point is to see movement rather than a single snapshot. Always check after a significant change: repositioning, new pricing, a new location, a rebrand. That is exactly where reality and what the model believes tend to diverge.

**Do reviews influence what AI says?**
Yes, noticeably. Models read the text, not just the score, so a detailed comment describing the job and the outcome is worth more than five stars with no words. The platform matters too: reviews on indexable sources like Google Business Profile and industry directories count, while closed internal surveys do not. Reviews also shape the wording - models often paraphrase what customers say about your strengths.

**What if the model confuses my company with another?**
That is an entity identification problem, and consistency fixes it. Make sure the site carries Organization markup with the full legal name, registration details, address and a sameAs list pointing to every official profile you have. Standardise the spelling of your name everywhere, including social and directories. If your name collides with a known brand from another industry, consistently pairing it with a qualifier in headings and copy helps.
