## Choosing a SaaS development company in Poland: freelancer, agency, or team?

- **A freelancer (me)** wins when you have a clear idea and want it in market fast, at a sane price, talking straight to the person writing the code. The honest downside: one person is one person. I manage that with a contract, staged payments, and everything on your accounts.
- **An agency** makes sense when you need many parallel workstreams - product, brand, ads, a big team - and budget is not the constraint. You pay for structure, and for a rotating cast where a junior often does the actual work.
- **A nearshore team** is for a funded company scaling an already-validated product with several developers at once. Overkill, and overpriced, for a first MVP whose entire point is to learn cheaply whether people want the thing.

For turning an idea into a launched SaaS in weeks, on a fixed budget, one senior is almost always the right first move. Scale the team after the market says yes.

**How long does it take to launch an MVP?**
A tightly scoped SaaS MVP with auth, one core flow, and billing is realistically 4 to 8 weeks with a single developer. More user roles, an admin panel, and outside integrations push it to 8 to 12 weeks. If someone promises a full SaaS in two weeks, you are getting a template that breaks the moment real users arrive.

**What features should the first version include?**
As few as possible: authentication, the one core flow your pitch is about, and billing only if charging is the thing you are testing. Cut admin analytics, team invites, social features, and dark mode from v1. Every extra screen is a week you are not learning whether people actually want the product.

Yes, completely. Source code, repository, database, and the Vercel and Stripe accounts are all in your name, confirmed in writing before we start. No lock-in, no hostage hosting. If you ever move the project elsewhere, the handover is a simple transfer, not a fight.

Yes, that is core to every SaaS I build. Stripe for checkout, subscriptions, plans, trials, and the webhooks behind them; secure auth with email and social sign-in; multi-tenant data isolation so accounts never see each other's data. It is all standard in my builds, not an expensive add-on.
