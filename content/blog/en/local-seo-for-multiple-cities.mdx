The typical situation: a company sits in Warsaw, works across Mazovia and three other cities, and only gets found on queries containing Warsaw. The reason is simple - to a search engine you exist where you have confirmed presence, and nowhere else.

Here is what to do, and more importantly how to avoid template city pages that no longer work and actively hurt.

## First, identify your business type

Everything else follows from this.

**A physical address in each city.** Office, salon, shop, pickup point. The simplest case: each location gets its own Google profile and you compete in each city's local results legitimately.

**One address, mobile service.** Repairs, installation, delivery, work at the client's site. Here the profile is configured as a service area business: the address is hidden and the served cities are listed. One profile covers several cities, but visibility in other cities will be weaker than a local competitor's.

**Remote work, nominal geography.** Development, consulting, online services. A service area profile still helps, but the main bet is the website and content, not maps.

## Google profile: what actually moves the needle

Local results and maps are the most stable channel for any business with a geography, and AI answers to local questions lean heavily on the same data.

What works:

- **The category** chosen precisely, not "broad just in case".
- **A full list of services** inside the profile, not just the name.
- **Reviews mentioning the city and the service** - the strongest signal. Ask clients to be specific: what you did and where.
- **Photos**, real and regularly added.
- **Opening hours** kept current, holidays included.
- **Replies to every review.**
- **Posts** in the profile at least monthly.

More on the profile in [this article](/en/blog/google-business-profile-rank-higher-on-maps).

What does not work: fake addresses in other cities. It breaks the rules, and the consequence is a suspended profile rather than growth.

## City pages on the website

This is where it gets interesting. The idea is obvious: a "service in city N" page for each city. The problem is the execution: people take one page, swap the city name and publish fifteen of them. Search engines treat those as doorway pages and either ignore them or demote the whole site.

A city page earns its place if it contains something the others do not. What can genuinely differ:

- **Real projects in that city** with photos and details.
- **Reviews from clients there.**
- **Terms specific to that city**: call-out time, delivery cost, service area, travel time.
- **Local specifics**: typical building stock, local requirements, market particulars, local partners.
- **Contacts**: a local phone number if you have one, a pickup address or landmark.
- **An FAQ with local questions** people actually ask.

Working rule: **if you can write fewer than 300 words of unique content about that city, do not build the page**. Three strong pages for three main cities beat fifteen empty ones.

## Structure: tying it together

The setup that works:

- **A main service page** with no geography - collects general queries and acts as the hub.
- **"Service in city" pages** - linking back to the main page and to each other via a "we also work in" block.
- **Case studies** - tied to cities and linking to the matching pages.
- **The blog** - pieces on local specifics linking to city pages.

Keep URLs readable: `/services/installation/warsaw`, not parameters like `?city=warsaw`.

## Local citations

What confirms your presence off-site:

- **Industry directories** and listings; in Poland that means Panorama Firm, Aleo, Oferteo and niche platforms.
- **Local business communities**, chambers of commerce, regional associations.
- **Partners in the city** whose supplier lists you can appear on.
- **Local media and blogs.**

Critical detail: name, address and phone must match everywhere, character for character. Inconsistent spellings are the most common reason local visibility stalls, and the reason language models answer about the company hesitantly.

## How this connects to AI answers

A separate argument for local work: for questions like "who does X in city Y", language models draw on map profiles, directories and reviews - the same data. A company with no local presence in those sources simply does not appear in the answer, however good its website. The mechanics are in [the GEO article](/en/blog/how-to-get-cited-by-chatgpt-and-ai-overviews).

## What to measure

- **Search Console by page**: impressions and clicks per city page individually. A page with no impressions after three months is a candidate for removal or rework.
- **Queries containing city names** in the queries report.
- **Profile insights**: calls, direction requests, website clicks, per location.
- **Enquiries tagged by city.** A simple form field or a bot question solves attribution.

## The order of work

1. Pick 3-5 priority cities from enquiry data, not ambition.
2. Configure the Google profile: separate profiles for addressed locations, service area for mobile work.
3. Collect material per city: projects, reviews, terms, specifics.
4. Build pages only where the material exists.
5. Set up internal linking and URL structure.
6. Accumulate local citations and reviews.
7. Review the data at three months and decide on further cities.

## FAQ

**Can I create a page for every city we serve?**
Only where you have something to say about each. Pages that differ solely by city name are treated as doorway pages and are either left unindexed or demoted along with the rest of the site. The working rule: if there is less than roughly 300 words of unique content - projects in that city, reviews from there, call-out terms, local specifics - skip the page. Three strong pages outperform fifteen empty ones.

**Do I need a separate Google profile for each city?**
Only where you have a real address there: an office, salon, shop or pickup point. If the work is mobile and there is one address, create a service area profile with the address hidden and the cities listed. Creating profiles at fictitious addresses is a policy violation that leads to suspension rather than more visibility.

**Why are we not found in the neighbouring city?**
Because presence is confirmed by data, not intent. If that city has no service-area profile, no page with local content, no reviews from clients there and no citations in local directories, there is no basis for showing you. Local results favour businesses with confirmed presence first.

**What should a city page contain so it does not look templated?**
Real projects in that city with photos, reviews from local clients, specific terms - call-out time, delivery cost, service area - local specifics such as typical building stock or requirements, local contacts, and an FAQ with the questions people genuinely ask there. The generic service copy should occupy a smaller share of the page than the local content.

**How does local SEO relate to AI answers?**
For questions like "who does X in city Y", language models rely on map profiles, local directories and reviews - the same sources as local search results. A company absent from that data does not appear in the answer regardless of website quality. So work on your profile, reviews and citations now pays twice: in maps and in AI answers.
