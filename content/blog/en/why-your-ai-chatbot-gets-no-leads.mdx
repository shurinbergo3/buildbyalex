The bot has been on the site for a month. Conversations are happening, the dashboard shows hundreds of messages, and enquiries are exactly where they were: nowhere. The usual conclusion follows - "AI does not work" - and the widget quietly disappears.

Nine times out of ten the problem is not the model but how the scenario is configured, or what happens to the enquiry after the conversation. Here are the causes by frequency, and the order to check them in.

## 1. The bot answers but never asks for a contact

The most common one. The bot answers the pricing question beautifully, the person says thanks and leaves. The conversation counts as successful, there is no enquiry.

A good scenario takes the next step after answering: offers a quote, a consultation, a recommendation, a booking, or to email the details. Not pushy, but always present. The formula is simple: **answer, then offer to continue**.

To check: open twenty conversations where the bot answered successfully. Count how many included an offer of a next step.

## 2. The knowledge base is too thin

The bot answers every second question with "please check with a colleague". The person concludes it is faster to message a competitor.

The signal: a high escalation rate and short conversations. If more than 60% of chats go to a human, the problem is not question complexity but that the bot has nothing to answer with.

The fix is material, not model configuration: prices, terms, timelines, common questions, service descriptions. How that works is in [the knowledge base bot article](/en/blog/ai-chatbot-with-knowledge-base).

## 3. The widget is invisible or badly timed

A small thing that undermines everything else. A chat button in the corner, grey on white, the size of an icon. Or the opposite: it pops up two seconds after load, covers the content, and gets reflexively closed.

What works: a visible button, an invitation after 15-30 seconds or on exit intent, and different invitation copy on different pages. On a pricing page, "Want help with a quote?" outperforms "Hello, how can I help?".

## 4. There is no way out to a human

The opposite extreme: the bot will not let go. Someone asks for a person three times and the bot keeps offering options. That irritates more than having no bot at all.

The button to reach a human should always be visible, and a direct request should trigger instantly. Covered in detail in [the escalation article](/en/blog/chatbot-handover-to-human-escalation).

## 5. The enquiry exists but never arrived

A technical cause, more common than anyone would like. The bot collected the contact, showed a thank-you, and then:

- the CRM integration broke a month ago and nobody noticed;
- notifications go to an inbox nobody reads;
- enquiries land in a section your team never opens;
- night-time enquiries are seen in the morning but not worked, because nothing flags them.

Five minutes to verify: walk the whole path as a customer, submit an enquiry and see where it lands and who finds out about it.

## 6. Nobody replies after the handover

The bot correctly passed the conversation to a person, and the person replied a day later. By then the customer had already messaged competitors.

Watch the time to first human response after escalation. If it exceeds an hour during working hours, the problem is not the bot.

## 7. There simply is not enough traffic

It happens: the bot works perfectly, but the site gets thirty visitors a day and five of them open the chat. With that base, enquiries will be few regardless of scenario quality.

Here it is not the bot that needs fixing. Related reading: [why your website gets no leads](/en/blog/why-website-gets-no-leads) and [what to do when traffic falls](/en/blog/organic-traffic-dropped-ai-overviews).

## The metrics that reveal it

Four numbers to check weekly.

**Share of visitors who start a conversation.** Below 2-3% and the problem is the widget and the invitation, not the answers.

**Share of conversations that leave a contact.** The key metric. If it is low, see point 1: the bot is not taking the next step.

**Escalation rate.** Healthy is 20-40%. Below 10% means the bot will not let go; above 60% means the knowledge base is weak.

**Rate of explicit "get me a human" requests.** Rising means the bot is irritating people.

And most importantly: **read twenty real conversations every week**. It is dull, takes half an hour, and tells you more than any report. You immediately see where the bot stalls, which questions go unanswered, and at what moment people leave.

## The diagnostic order

If there are no enquiries, check in this order - cheapest first.

1. **Submit an enquiry yourself** and follow it through to the CRM and the notification. Five minutes.
2. **Check the widget is visible** on mobile across three key pages. Five minutes.
3. **Read twenty conversations.** Half an hour.
4. **Calculate the four metrics** above. An hour.
5. **Check human response time** after escalation.
6. **Look at traffic**: how many people actually reach the pages where the bot lives.

The cause is usually found in the first three steps.

## What to do next

Fixes in order of return:

- Add a next step after the answer - an offer of a quote, consultation or booking.
- Add to the knowledge base whatever the bot could not answer in the conversations you read.
- Repair the CRM link and notifications, and route them somewhere they will definitely be seen - usually Telegram.
- Make the widget visible and tune invitations per page.
- Add a button to reach a human if there is none.
- Configure a night-time scenario: an honest message about response time plus contact capture.

The first three usually take a day or two and deliver most of the effect.

## FAQ

**Why does my chatbot answer well but generate no leads?**
Usually because the scenario ends at the answer. The bot closed the pricing question, the person said thanks and left. The working formula is different: answer first, then offer a next step - a quote, a consultation, a recommendation, a booking or emailing the details. You can verify this in half an hour: open twenty successful conversations and count how many included an offer to continue.

**What share of visitors should start a chat?**
As a benchmark, 2-3% of site visitors and up, depending on niche and traffic. Below that, the cause is usually the widget itself: an unnoticeable button, an invitation appearing too early and covering content, or invitation copy unrelated to the page. On a pricing page an offer to help with a quote performs noticeably better than a generic greeting.

**How do I know whether the problem is the bot or the traffic?**
Build the funnel: how many people reach pages with the bot, how many start a conversation, how many leave a contact, how many become deals. Plenty of conversations but few contacts means the scenario is the problem. Almost no conversations at normal traffic means the widget is. If traffic itself is thin, fixing the bot is pointless and acquisition comes first.

**What if the bot answers everything with "check with a colleague"?**
That is not a model problem, it is missing material. Gather what the knowledge base lacks: prices and ranges, terms, timelines, answers to common questions, service descriptions, limitations. The fastest way to find the gaps is to read a week of conversations and write down the questions that went unanswered. It is usually a list of twenty items, and closing it changes the picture.

**Where should bot enquiries land?**
In the CRM with the full conversation history, plus an instant notification somewhere the team will definitely see it - in practice Telegram rather than a shared inbox. Night and weekend enquiries deserve their own flag so they do not blend into the morning queue. And verify the link yourself: integrations break silently, and people usually find out a month later from the absence of leads.
