## Real-world case: a components manufacturer near Katowice

A company in Upper Silesia - they manufacture metal components for industry and export to Germany and the Czech Republic - came to me with a ten-year-old WordPress site. It loaded in six or seven seconds, the catalog was a heap of PDFs, the layout broke on a phone, and there was no English version at all. That last part matters more than it sounds: buyers at German plants google their suppliers from a phone, and the first impression of a factory is formed on its website.

The concrete pain was leads, or the lack of them. The contact form was buried in the footer and some of its emails vanished on the way. The sales team kept having to explain from scratch, in every meeting, what the company could actually do, because there was nothing to point at. And before the fairs at Międzynarodowe Targi Katowickie it stung the most - a partner would take a business card, open the site that evening, and find an artifact from 2015.

I built a new site on Next.js: a structured product catalog with filters by type and material, a clear page for each product group, and a one-screen inquiry form that drops straight into their CRM. PL and EN versions, both with properly proofread technical copy - for export that's not optional, a buyer shouldn't trip over a clumsy translation. Separately I went after performance: LCP under a second, Core Web Vitals green on mobile.

Three months after launch, inquiries were up roughly threefold - from a trickle to 12-15 a month - LCP was under one second against the old 6-plus, and about a third of the inquiries came in English, which meant the export side started arriving on its own instead of only through cold outreach. The sales team stopped explaining things in the air and began sending links to specific pages. The project took four weeks.
