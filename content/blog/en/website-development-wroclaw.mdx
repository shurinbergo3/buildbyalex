## What actually drives Google rankings (and what doesn't)

Most Wrocław businesses spend their budget on the wrong half of the site. They pay for animations and a hero video. Then they wonder why a competitor with an uglier site sits above them.

Here's what Google actually rewards.

**Speed.** You tap a search result on your phone, walking past the fountain at Plac Grunwaldzki. Three seconds pass. Still loading. You hit back and tap the next result. Google logged every millisecond of that. Core Web Vitals — LCP, INP, CLS — are a ranking factor, and a slow site bleeds traffic before anyone reads a word. Most Wrocław traffic comes off a phone, often on patchy transit signal. Build for that.

**Local SEO.** A Google Business Profile that's actually filled out. `LocalBusiness` schema in the code. Phrasing that matches how people search — "web design Wrocław," "dentist Krzyki," "law firm Wrocław centrum." If you serve a district, name the district. Google's local pack is its own little battlefield and most sites never even enter it.

**Clean, parseable HTML.** This is the new one. People don't only search on Google anymore — they ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google's AI Overviews. Those models read your raw HTML. A site buried under script-rendered junk and plugin bloat is hard for them to quote. A clean static site gets cited. I've watched this happen with my own builds in the last year.

What doesn't move rankings: parallax scrolling, a video background, a "unique" font nobody can read, and the seventeenth animation on the homepage. Spend that money on speed and content instead.

## Next.js vs WordPress — the Wrocław reality

This is the biggest technical call you'll make, and in a city packed with developers you'll get strong opinions both directions.

**WordPress** still earns its place. If you've got an in-house editorial team that lives in the admin panel and publishes weekly — or your budget sits at the lower end — it's defensible. The catch: speed has to be forced with caching plugins, security needs constant patching, and it's weak for the AI-search reading I just described. A neglected WordPress site is a hacked WordPress site within a year.

**Next.js** (what I build on) ships fast by default. Static rendering, Core Web Vitals green from day one, clean HTML that Google and the AI models both parse without choking. Hosting on Vercel is often free. For a company site, a landing page, or anything aimed at ranking, it's the better foundation in 2026.

Simple rule: content-heavy site with a daily-publishing team → WordPress is fine. Everything else aimed at ranking and AI search → Next.js. If you want the full scope and example builds, [see the website development service](/en/services/websites).
