The most common mistake after launch is deciding the work is finished. The site is live, the link went into the group chat, everyone congratulated each other. A month later there are no enquiries, and the verdict becomes "the site is bad". In reality the site may be excellent - nobody knows about it, and half the technical plumbing was never connected.

Here is the 90-day plan I hand clients when a project ships.

## Week 1: technical hygiene

Dull and non-negotiable. All of it is done once and then works for years.

**Check indexing.** Run `site:yourdomain` in Google. If nothing appears after a few days, check `robots.txt` and meta tags. The classic culprit is a `noindex` left over from staging. I find it regularly.

**Connect Search Console** and submit the sitemap. It is the only source of truth about what the search engine sees. Add Bing Webmaster Tools too - its data feeds other services, including parts of AI search.

**Set up analytics with conversions.** Not just a page counter but events: form submissions, phone clicks, messenger clicks, completed orders. Without them, in a month you will not be able to say whether the site works. How to do it is in [the GA4 article](/en/blog/how-to-track-conversions-ga4).

**Test every form.** Submit each one and confirm the email arrived, the notification fired, the enquiry reached the CRM and the visitor saw a confirmation. A form that silently loses enquiries is the most expensive mistake on this list.

**Set up redirects** if there was an old site. Every old URL must point to its new equivalent. Skip this and you reset rankings built over years.

**Measure speed** on mobile and record the numbers as a baseline.

**Walk the customer journey on a phone**: homepage, service page, form, submission. On a real device, not an emulator.

**Configure backups** and uptime monitoring.

## Weeks 2-4: seed demand

Search engines do not know you yet. So your first clients will not come from organic.

**Google Business Profile**, if you have a geography. It is the fastest source of enquiries for a local business: a complete profile starts generating calls in weeks, not months. What to fill in is in [ranking higher on Maps](/en/blog/google-business-profile-rank-higher-on-maps).

**Directories and profiles.** Industry listings, LinkedIn, social. The point is less about traffic and more about corroborating that the company exists - which language models need too, as covered in [what AI says about your company](/en/blog/what-chatgpt-says-about-your-company).

**Ads to test hypotheses.** A small Google Ads budget for two or three weeks is the fastest way to learn whether the offer lands and the page converts. Cheaper than waiting three months for organic and only then discovering the homepage copy is wrong.

**First reviews.** Ask existing clients to leave a Google review. Three to five genuine reviews in month one do more than any badge on the site.

**Write 3-5 pieces** answering specific client questions. Not "about us", but the things people actually ask: prices, timelines, how to choose, what the differences are.

## Month 2: read the data and fix

The first numbers arrive, and now you can work with them.

**Search Console, the queries report.** Look at which phrasings you are being shown for. There are almost always surprises: queries you never considered that are already generating impressions. That tells you which pages to strengthen.

**Pages with impressions but no clicks.** The snippet is not landing: rewrite title and description.

**On-site behaviour.** Where people leave, whether they reach the form, how long they spend on key pages.

**First enquiries.** Where they came from and what they ask. Recurring questions belong on the site: it improves conversion and adds the kind of content that gets cited.

**Technical errors** from Search Console: broken links, error pages, mobile issues.

## Month 3: scale up

By month three it is clear what works. Now you amplify.

**Regular content.** One or two pieces a month answering real questions. No more than that; quality beats volume, especially now that shallow articles have lost their purpose, as covered in [the traffic drop article](/en/blog/organic-traffic-dropped-ai-overviews).

**Pages for commercial queries.** Prices, cities, specific services. Those are what generate enquiries.

**Off-site mentions.** Partners, industry platforms, case studies with clients.

**Conversion work.** There is finally enough traffic to see bottlenecks and change things deliberately.

**An AI agent on the site** once contact volume is meaningful. It answers instantly and captures enquiries while the visitor is still on the page - details on [the service page](/en/services/ai-agents).

## What to realistically expect

Honest expectations, to avoid disappointment:

- **Indexing**: a few days to two weeks.
- **First search impressions**: 2-4 weeks.
- **First organic enquiries**: 1-3 months, on low-competition and branded queries.
- **Rankings on competitive queries**: 4-8 months and beyond.
- **Enquiries from ads**: from week one, if the offer and landing page are in order.

A brand-new domain with no history tracks the upper end of those ranges. A domain with history tracks the lower end.

## Five expensive mistakes

- **No redirects from the old site.** Rankings reset, and it stings because it takes an hour to fix.
- **A forgotten `noindex`** from staging.
- **No conversion tracking.** Three months later, nobody can say what worked.
- **Expecting the site to promote itself.** It will not; it needs mentions and content.
- **Changing the URL structure** a month after launch without redirects - a second reset.

## FAQ

**How long before a new website starts bringing in clients?**
From organic search, first enquiries typically appear within 1-3 months on low-competition and branded queries, while rankings on competitive terms take 4-8 months. From ads, enquiries arrive in week one if the offer and landing page are solid. So the sensible approach is to cover the first two months with paid channels and a local profile while organic accumulates.

**What should I do immediately after launch?**
Check indexing with a site:yourdomain query, connect Search Console and submit the sitemap, set up analytics with conversion events, test every form through to the CRM, add redirects from the old site, measure mobile speed and walk the customer journey on a real phone. That is a week of work, and it determines whether everything else is worth doing.

**Why is my new site not appearing in Google?**
The most common causes: the site is blocked from indexing by a meta tag or robots.txt left over from staging; the sitemap was never submitted; pages render only in the browser so the crawler gets an empty shell; or the domain is brand new and crawling has not reached it. Check Search Console, which shows which pages were crawled and which were excluded and why.

**Do I need ads if I am doing SEO?**
In the first months, yes, and not only for the enquiries. Ads quickly reveal whether the offer lands and the page converts, whereas organic answers that question six months later. A small test budget for two or three weeks saves months of guesswork. You can then taper the ads as the organic channel picks up.

**How do I know the site is working when enquiries are still thin?**
Look at intermediate signals rather than enquiry count alone. In Search Console: whether impressions are growing and on which queries, and whether there are pages with impressions but no clicks. In analytics: whether people reach the form, how long they spend on key pages, where they come from. If impressions grow but enquiries do not, the problem is page conversion rather than visibility, and that is faster to fix.
