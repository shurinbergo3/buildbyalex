## Real-world case: a fashion brand in Łódź

A clothing brand selling through their own WooCommerce store, Allegro, and a B2B wholesale channel - orders arriving by email from regular buyers. Three sources, three data formats. Every morning one person pulled everything into a shared spreadsheet, updated stock counts and sent pick tasks to the warehouse. At 40-80 orders a day that took close to two hours, and overselling still happened because stock sold on Allegro was too slow to disappear from the shop view.

What we did. I wired three sources into one n8n workflow: a WooCommerce webhook, the Allegro API, and a parser for wholesale emails. Each order normalizes to a single format, lands in the management system, updates stock across all channels at once, and sends a pick task to the warehouse team via Telegram. The workflow runs around the clock - an order placed at 11 PM is ready to fulfill at 11 PM, not at 8 AM the next morning.

The result after the first month: the morning copy-paste routine was gone entirely. The same team now handles twice the order volume without overselling. The build took two weeks and paid back within the first quarter - the reduction in mis-sold stock alone covered the cost.

## n8n or Make

I use both tools - the choice depends on the project.

**n8n** - when you need data to stay inside your own infrastructure, or when predictable costs matter regardless of how many operations run. Self-hosted, no per-run pricing. Good fit for fashion and e-commerce companies with sensitive buyer data.

**Make** - when a fast start and a wide library of ready-made connectors matter more. At low volumes that is €20-€60 per month.

For most Łódź businesses, Make is fine to start with. When volume grows or data residency matters, n8n pays off.

**Which processes should I automate first?**
Ones that are repetitive, structured and consume time across several people. Common starting points: lead handling from a form, invoice generation after an order, stock level sync across sales channels. One condition: the process must already work correctly - automation speeds up a functioning process, it does not fix a broken one.

**n8n, Make or Zapier - which tool should I choose?**
Zapier is the simplest but the most expensive at scale - at 5,000 tasks per month that is over $300. Make is 3-4 times cheaper for the same volume and has solid connectors for WooCommerce, Allegro and popular CRM tools. n8n runs on your own server - data stays in your infrastructure, costs are flat regardless of volume. For e-commerce and fashion companies with sensitive buyer data, n8n is often the right call.

**What is the ROI of process automation?**
Typical ROI is 70-400% in year one, with payback in 4-9 months. A simple formula: hours saved per month × hourly rate × 12 > implementation cost. Example: 10 hours per month × €15/h × 12 = €1 800 per year. If the project cost €900, it pays back in 6 months.

**Is automation GDPR-compliant?**
Yes, when data is processed on EU servers. n8n self-hosted on a Polish server keeps all data in your infrastructure and does not send it to external providers in the US. Configuration includes encryption, a data retention policy and GDPR compliance from the start.

**Where do I start with automation in a small company?**
With one process that has the highest ratio of "time lost per day / difficulty to automate." Most common first wins: invoice generation after an order, new lead notifications, stock sync between sales channels. You see results within the first week - that is a good moment to decide what to automate next.
