Hospitality has two permanent pains, and one tool addresses both. First: platform commission eats a meaningful slice of every booking. Second: guests message around the clock in several languages, while the front desk answers during office hours in two.

An AI agent closes both. Here is what it actually does in a hotel, an aparthotel and a short-term rental, and how to calculate the effect.

## Where the money leaks

**Platform commission.** It is baked into the price, and every booking that arrives direct is margin that stays with you. The bot's job is not to replace the platforms but to capture the people who already found you there and came to your site to check.

**Night-time enquiries.** Questions about availability peak in the evening and at night, when people plan trips. A morning reply is often too late: the guest booked with whoever answered immediately.

**Language barrier.** Guests write in English, German, Ukrainian, Russian. A multilingual front desk is expensive; answering through a translator is slow.

**Repeating questions.** Check-in, check-out, parking, breakfast, pets, transfer, luggage, wifi. Dozens of identical messages a day, taking time from tasks where a person is genuinely needed.

## What the bot does

**Answers accommodation questions** instantly and in the guest's language: room descriptions, what is included, terms, rules, facilities, how to get there.

**Shows availability and rates.** Connected to the property management system, the bot sees genuinely free dates and gives a concrete answer rather than "please check". How system access works is in [the MCP article](/en/blog/mcp-connect-ai-agent-to-your-systems).

**Drives the direct booking.** Picks an option, explains the difference and hands over to your booking flow. That is where the commission saving appears.

**Prepares the guest for arrival.** The day before: how to get there, where to park, check-in time, how to get the keys, who to message if something goes wrong.

**Serves guests during the stay.** An extra towel, late checkout, city recommendations, a taxi. The guest writes in chat instead of looking for the front desk.

**Sells extras.** Late checkout, breakfast, parking, transfers, tours. Unobtrusively and at the right moment.

**Asks for a review after checkout.** Automatically, at the right time, with a direct link. Reviews affect both your platform ranking and what AI assistants say about you - covered in [the brand mentions article](/en/blog/what-chatgpt-says-about-your-company).

## Apartments and short-term rentals

The effect is even more visible here, because there is no front desk at all.

An owner with several apartments physically cannot answer around the clock, and that is exactly when guests write. The bot takes the whole cycle: pre-booking questions, check-in instructions with codes and address, in-stay questions, checkout reminder, review request. A person steps in only where a decision is needed: a problem with the flat, a disputed situation, an unusual request.

For management companies with dozens of units, that is the difference between "two people on messages" and "one person on exceptions".

## What gets connected

- **The property management system or channel manager** - for availability and rates.
- **The booking engine** on your site - so the bot can carry people through to payment.
- **Messengers**: WhatsApp and Telegram, where guests write most.
- **Payments** - for deposits and extras.
- **CRM or guest database** - so returning guests are recognised.

You do not need all of it at once. Sensibly, start with the website and one messenger plus a property knowledge base, and add the management system integration as step two.

## How to calculate the effect

Three sources.

**Commission saved.** If the bot converts even a modest share of chat enquiries into direct bookings, the difference versus a platform is immediate and usually covers the build within a season.

**Recovered night-time enquiries.** Count how many messages arrive outside front desk hours and how many currently wait until morning. Even a portion of those, converted, is direct revenue.

**Staff relief.** Routine questions consume hours. The calculation method is the same as in [the manager replacement article](/en/blog/ai-agent-vs-human-manager-real-math).

## Important limits

Stating these plainly so expectations stay realistic.

The bot **will not replace platforms as an acquisition channel**: people still search there. It captures those who already reached your site or messaged you directly.

The bot **does not resolve conflicts**. An unhappy guest, a refund dispute, a problem in the room - straight to a person.

The bot **must identify itself honestly** as an AI assistant. Since August 2026 that is also an AI Act requirement - covered in [this article](/en/blog/eu-ai-act-chatbot-requirements-2026).

And most importantly: the bot is useless if your site cannot take a direct booking. First a working booking engine and a proper [website](/en/services/websites), then the bot.

## FAQ

**Will a bot replace the hotel front desk?**
No, and that should not be the goal. It closes routine questions about rooms, terms, arrival and facilities, shows availability and drives direct bookings, while handing complex situations, conflicts and unusual requests to a person. The main benefit is not headcount reduction but that answers arrive instantly at any hour, including at night, which is exactly when guests plan trips.

**How does a bot increase direct bookings?**
It captures guests who found the property on a platform but came to your site to compare. At that moment the bot answers their questions, shows free dates and carries them through to a direct booking. No platform commission is paid on that booking, and that difference is usually what covers the build. The bot cannot replace platforms as an acquisition channel; it works on converting those who already reached you.

**Does this work for apartments and short-term rentals?**
Yes, and the effect is even more visible because there is no front desk at all. The bot handles the whole cycle: pre-booking questions, check-in instructions with codes and address, in-stay questions, checkout reminder and the review request. An owner with several flats only steps in for exceptions rather than answering the same questions around the clock.

**Which languages does the bot answer in?**
Whichever language the guest writes in, from a single knowledge base. That is one of the biggest advantages in hospitality: a multilingual front desk is expensive, while a bot answers in English, German, Ukrainian and Polish with equal accuracy, because it forms the answer from your material rather than translating canned phrases.

**What does a hotel bot cost?**
A basic version with a property knowledge base, answers and enquiry capture on the site and one messenger is €1,200-2,500 and about two weeks. With integration to the management system, live availability and booking handoff, €2,500-5,000 and 3-4 weeks. For a group or management company with several properties and upsells, from €5,000. Plus a moderate monthly fee for model usage.
